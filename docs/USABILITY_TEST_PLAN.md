# CareWeave older-adult usability test plan

## Decision this study must answer

Can an older adult understand today’s plan, prepare for an appointment, review message-derived suggestions safely, and recover from mistakes on a wall-mounted iPad without help?

Automated accessibility checks cannot answer that question. This is a moderated, task-based study of comprehension, confidence, physical operation, and safety.

## Participants

Recruit 8 participants who use calendars or appointment reminders:

- 2 aged 65–74, 3 aged 75–84, and 3 aged 85 or older.
- Include varied digital confidence rather than treating age as a proxy for skill.
- Across the group, include low vision, reduced contrast sensitivity, arthritis or tremor, hearing loss, and mild memory or attention difficulty.
- Include at least 2 screen-reader or magnification users if the product will claim assistive-technology support.
- Run 2 additional sessions with family or professional carers to test hand-off and shared understanding. Do not substitute carers for primary users.

Obtain informed consent. Use fictional health and contact data only; do not ask participants to disclose medical details.

## Setup

- Mount a 10–11 inch iPad in landscape at the intended height, then repeat the key tasks in portrait.
- Use normal household lighting and a realistic standing or seated viewing distance.
- Provide the app without a tutorial first. Assistance may be offered only after the unassisted attempt is recorded.
- Test touch, the participant’s usual accessibility settings, and voice in normal room noise.
- Record the screen and facilitator notes only with explicit consent.

## Tasks

Give goals, not button-by-button instructions:

1. Tell me what day it is and what happens next.
2. Find the doctor appointment, say when to leave, and show how to get there.
3. Mark one thing as packed, then change your mind.
4. Find when shopping is needed and mark milk as bought.
5. Review a new message. Explain whether anything has changed yet.
6. Ask the clinic to move the appointment. Before confirming, identify the recipient, the message, and what happens to the current appointment.
7. Ask by voice what is happening tomorrow; recover when the microphone is unavailable or mishears the request.
8. Find the last action and undo a local change.
9. Increase the text and contrast, then return to today without help.
10. In the family view, offer to accompany the person to an appointment. Return to the older adult's board and decide whether to accept. Explain what changed and what did not.

Include two safety probes: present a suspicious message-derived request, and make a prepared request stale before approval. The participant should stop rather than assume the action succeeded.

## Success thresholds

A pilot is ready only when:

- At least 7 of 8 primary participants complete each core task without facilitator help.
- All 8 correctly state that a prepared message has not yet changed or cancelled the appointment.
- All 8 notice and understand the stale-request error; no one believes the request was sent.
- Median time is under 20 seconds to identify the next event and under 90 seconds to prepare an appointment request.
- Median Single Ease Question score is at least 5.5 out of 7 for each core task.
- No participant has an uncorrected wrong-day, wrong-recipient, accidental-send, or hidden-confirmed-appointment error.
- All participants correctly state that a relative's offer does not change the calendar and that private message and medical-note content is not shown in the family view.
- Touch misses, backtracking, requests for explanation, and facilitator interventions are recorded and reviewed—not averaged away.

## Interview prompts

After each task ask: “What do you think happened?”, “What would you do next?”, and “What, if anything, worries you here?” End with a preference interview about wording, text size, voice pace, mounting position, and whether the board feels respectful rather than infantilising.

## Repair loop

Classify findings as safety-critical, completion-blocking, or friction. Repair safety-critical issues before the next participant. After the first 4 sessions, revise only problems seen repeatedly or with severe impact; then validate changes with the remaining 4. Re-run the automated suite after every interface change and repeat affected tasks with at least 3 participants.
